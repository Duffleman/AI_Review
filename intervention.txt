intervention [string]: Name of the intervention being evaluated, as the paper names it (e.g. a programme or app name). If it has no name, give a short descriptive label (e.g. internet-based CBT for insomnia). If several active interventions are compared, name each, separated by '; '. Do not include the control condition.
guided [boolean]: true if participants received support from a human (e.g. therapist, coach, clinician or trained lay person giving feedback, messages, calls or sessions) as part of the intervention; false if the intervention is self-guided, i.e. fully automated or used without human support. Automated reminders and purely technical support do not count as guidance. If the study includes both guided and unguided versions, return true.
target [string]: The condition or problem the intervention is designed to address. If the paper names a diagnostic label (e.g. major depressive disorder, generalised anxiety disorder), give that. Otherwise give a brief description of the primary symptom target (e.g. elevated depressive symptoms, perceived stress). A few words only.
developer [string]: Who developed or released the intervention, as stated in the paper, e.g. the authors' research group, a named university, a company or a health service. If the paper says it was adapted from an existing programme, state the original developer and who adapted it.
length [string]: Intended duration and dose of the intervention: number of modules or sessions, total time span (e.g. weeks), and recommended pace or session length if given (e.g. 8 modules over 10 weeks, one module per week, about 30 minutes each).
content [string]: Description of what the intervention contains: the therapeutic approach (e.g. CBT, mindfulness), delivery format (e.g. web-based, smartphone app, group sessions), main components or module topics, and, if guided, what the human support involved. Two to four sentences.
