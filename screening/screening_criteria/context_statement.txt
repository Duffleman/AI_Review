For the purposes of the current study, we define digital mental health interventions as:
1. Structured programs comprising standardized therapeutic content, which may be delivered independently as self-help programs or with support from a therapist or other professional (including synchronous or asynchronous guidance);
2. Delivered via digital platforms, including but not limited to web-based systems, mobile applications, or other connected technologies;
3. Primarily aimed at the prevention, treatment, or management of mental health symptoms or disorders. We explicitly exclude telemedicine, including one-on-one therapy delivered via telephone or videoconferencing, as this constitutes a mode of delivering traditional services rather than a digital intervention in and of itself. Similarly, we exclude the use of digital tools such as ecological momentary assessment, automated patient reported outcome systems, biofeedback, chatbots, or digital screening instruments when used solely as adjuncts to routine face-to-face care, as these do not, in isolation, constitute an intervention.
